# Prior work, and what is different here

| Work | What it does | How self-steer differs |
|---|---|---|
| [Weight-Space Programming](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/) (Takehi, Teoh, Zhang, Khattab; MIT, Nov 2026) | A frontier model reads raw weights and edits Qwen3.5-0.8B: it removes one direction from 64 MLP output columns in each of two layers (0.016% of weights). That fixes reasoning loops; MATH-500 goes from 7.9% to 48.1%. | Their edits are offline, with a budget of forward passes per session. Here, edits are switched per token while the model is running, and the model chooses its own. |
| WeAct, reported in [a blog post](https://kenashe.ai/blog/2026-10-20-weact-turns-lora-adapters-into-something-an-agent-can-pick-mid-task) (Oct 2026; paper not found) | A Qwen3-32B agent loads skill LoRA adapters through tool calls, between steps. | It needs trained adapters. Here, edits are small and interpretable (neuron doses, single directions), and a controller can create new ones in seconds. |
| [Transformer²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06252) (Sakana AI, 2025) | A first pass identifies the task; a second pass mixes SVF expert vectors that scale singular values. | Dispatch is fixed at two passes, and the experts are trained with RL. Here, the model decides mid-conversation through ordinary tool calls. |
| [SEAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10943) (MIT, NeurIPS 2025) | The model writes its own fine-tuning data ("self-edits") and updates its weights through SFT. | SEAL's updates are persistent and slow. Edits here are reversible and immediate. |
| [Text-to-LoRA / Doc-to-LoRA](https://sakana.ai/doc-to-lora/) (Sakana AI, 2025–26) | A hypernetwork turns a task description or document into a LoRA in one forward pass. | That needs a meta-trained hypernetwork. Here, a frontier model writes contrastive examples, and a mean difference becomes the edit. |
| [CAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05907) (IBM, ICLR 2025) | A condition vector over the model's own activations decides whether a fixed steering vector applies. | Here, the model chooses the edit explicitly and can ask for new ones. |
| [CAA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06681) (Rimsky et al., 2023); [ActAdd](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10248) (Turner et al., 2023) | Steering vectors built from mean activation differences. | self-steer uses CAA as its quick builder for new modes. |
| [Prompt-Activation Duality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10664) (Kang et al., May 2026) | Shows that steering residual states in multi-turn dialogue contaminates the KV cache and causes coherence drift. | Experiment E3 measures this directly for switched edits, with and without re-encoding the cache. |
| [KV Cache Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08799) (2025) | Steers by editing the cache once after prefill. | — |
| Lazy Genius Qwen ([21J3phy/lazy-genius-qwen](https://github.com/21J3phy/lazy-genius-qwen)) | 344 learned neuron doses applied over the first 6 answer tokens make Qwen3.5-4B 37% shorter. Tested in two pre-registered blind tests. | That edit is the built-in `lazy` mode here, reproduced token for token. |
